Table2 — Severity: MEDIUM. Two substantive clarifications requested.

T2.1 — The memory/representation row does not specify which controlled comparisons are actually intended.
Evidence visible in the table: the variants include "Sequential interaction evidence with a sequence plan," "graph plan without retrieval," and "instruction-only, state-only, and full graph retrieval." The interpretation says to separate historical evidence access, plan structure, and query information, while the caption says individual comparisons vary only the listed mechanism. However, the variants do not say whether instruction-only/state-only refer to retrieval queries, retrieved content, or planner observations, nor do they identify the matched contrast for each factor. In particular, sequence evidence with a sequence plan versus a graph plan without retrieval would change both evidence access and plan structure if compared directly.
Why it matters: a standalone reader cannot determine which contrast would support an effect of graph representation rather than access to history or query information. The general promise of matched source corpus does not establish matched evidence supplied to the planner.
Concrete fix: split this row into explicit pairwise contrasts, or add compact factor columns for history access, plan representation, and retrieval-query information. Define instruction-only and state-only locally. For a representation-only contrast, state that both conditions receive the same node evidence and predicates/relations (with their encoding varied); for a retrieval-access contrast, hold plan representation fixed. Do not imply that every pair in the current list is controlled.

T2.2 — The first two adaptation variants are not distinguishable under the stated common control.
Evidence visible in the table: variants are "Role rebinding only" and "rebinding plus current-scene parameters," but the interpretation states that every executable variant grounds current geometry and checks capabilities.
Why it matters: current-scene parameter grounding sounds like the same operation required of both variants. Without a division of responsibility, the reader cannot identify the treatment added by the second condition or know whether the first condition is an invalid transfer baseline.
Concrete fix: name the parameters that vary and the grounding that is common to every condition. For example, if correct, specify that the local executor supplies identical mandatory live-pose/feasibility grounding in all conditions, while the second condition additionally adapts named planner-level parameters such as approach choice, clearance, or grasp strategy. Use the actual intended variables, not these examples unless accurate. If no additional parameter intervention exists, merge the two variants. Preserve the useful prohibition on replaying incompatible absolute trajectories.

Other Table2 rows: relations/feedback distinguish information removal from lossless text encoding; the hierarchy controls identify shared controllers, plans, branches, and escalation budgets; the prior-work-inspired status is appropriately qualified. No additional mandatory issue found. Bracketed references need not supply operational definitions for the stated mechanism-level summary.

Table3 — Severity: MEDIUM. Clarify the historical comparison condition.

T3.1 — "Fixed context" has no standalone operational definition.
Evidence visible in the table/caption: dual-memory retrieval selects up to eight training and four current-episode chunks, while fixed context is only named. The same corpus, controller source, harness, and model are listed, but the fixed condition's actual content, selection rule, and size/budget are not specified.
Why it matters: the two conditions are the principal independent variable of this measured pilot. The reader cannot tell whether the fixed context contains training examples, current-episode history, both, or neither, or whether changing context quantity accompanies changing selection. This limits interpretation even though the table correctly makes no general graph-method claim.
Concrete fix: add one concise definition of the fixed condition's content and how it was chosen, and state whether prompt/context budgets were matched. If they were not matched, state that explicitly as a descriptive pilot limitation. Include the fixed chunk/token count if available; do not invent it. If the historical configuration cannot be recovered, say so and narrow the comparison to the recorded conditions.

Otherwise Table3 is clear: each row is one rollout; the two layouts give two paired comparisons; progress is explicitly a native score out of 100, completion is a separate native flag, steps are executed simulator actions, and calls are completed model requests. Development versus validation status is stated. The opposite directions of the action/call differences are acknowledged. No success-rate denominator, uncertainty interval, or significance claim is being implied by this four-row descriptive table, so adding such statistics is not requested.
